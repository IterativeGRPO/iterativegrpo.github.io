Multi-turn RL with Step-Level Q-functions

Batch-online multi-turn RL via single-turn RL. A step-level Qπ forecasts the environment’s expected downstream return for each response, without simulating possibly random trajectories during policy improvement.

Read the paper ↗ View the code →

Daniel R. Jiang · Ankur Samanta · Yukai Yang · Jalaj Bhandari · Rémi Munos · Tyler Lu

01 / The setting

Multi-turn policy improvement without a simulator in the training loop.

Many conversational agents are designed to drive outcomes such as resolving a support issue, completing a task, or helping a customer reach a decision. These interactions unfold over multiple turns, and success may only be known after the full conversation ends.

Batch-online deployment

Training proceeds in deployment rounds.

Real-world deployment is rarely fully online or fully offline. In the batch-online setting, the current policy runs over a deployment window—such as an A/B test lasting days or weeks—and logs complete conversations with observed outcomes. Training then happens offline on that fixed batch before the updated policy is deployed again.

A On-policy multi-turn RL

The environment must respond after every agent turn.

R

Full-trajectory policy optimization requires an interactive environment inside the training loop.

B Iterative GRPO

Qπ forecasts the environment's expected downstream return.

stateresponseQπ

Each candidate response can be scored without a simulator generating the remaining conversation.

02 / The method

Forecast the environment's expected downstream return.

At each deployment round, I-GRPO learns a step-level Q-function Qπ from logged trajectories. It forecasts the expected downstream return from the environment for each candidate response. Here, one step is one agent response conditioned on the full conversation history. I-GRPO then runs GRPO using Qπ as the reward model, without querying a simulator during policy improvement.

03 / Evaluation suite

Six multi-turn negotiation environments.

The suite spans distributive bargaining, resource allocation, and multi-issue agreement. Each environment changes what the parties know, what they can trade, and what counts as a successful outcome.

04 / Results

Held-out evaluation.

I-GRPO achieves the highest held-out expected discounted return in five of six environments and improves over the initial policy in all six. Results use four deployment rounds, 100 in-training kernels per environment, and four conversations per kernel.
